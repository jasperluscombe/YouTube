# Deep research: why some new channels explode and we don't (2026-11-13)

Method: public YouTube listings via yt-dlp (search + each channel's Shorts/Videos tab), auto-captions of the top
videos, plus web searches on the Shorts/TikTok algorithms. I could read titles, stats and transcripts but could NOT
watch the videos or see thumbnails (video/image hosts are blocked from this environment). TikTok/Instagram can't be
listed from here, so this is YouTube evidence.

## 1. The baseline (honest)
Most new channels in animated survival/zombie Shorts get tens to low thousands of views per Short:
Survive the Dead (median 239), Cool Stick (301), StickFallout (608), AfterZero (479), Rank Rumble (34), Mind Snacks (25).
We get ~150-700 per post per platform (~2,000 per story). That is normal, not broken. The channels below are the outliers.

## 3. What the 7.2M video does that ours don't (from its transcript)
Opening line: "If the zombie apocalypse starts while you're on a farm, don't run to town..."
1. **61 seconds, 191 words = ~190 words/min.** Ours: 120-150 s, ~250 words = ~110 words/min with dramatic pauses.
   Our average watch time is 5-14 s, i.e. viewers see under 10% of a 2-minute video. A 60 s video with the same
   opening holds a far bigger share, and "viewed vs swiped away" + completion are what Shorts/TikTok distribute on.
2. **The hook is a decision for the viewer**, not a mood: "If X happens while you're at Y, don't do Z." The viewer
   instantly argues with it ("why not?") and stays for the answer.
3. **Every line is a concrete survival decision with a consequence** (well pump dies with the power -> generator ->
   noise carries hundreds of metres -> 30 chickens, 12 cattle, a dog that barks). This is the "I should've thought
   of that" feeling. No backstory, no scene-setting, no adjectives.
4. **Specific numbers everywhere** (30 minutes, 30 chickens, 3 miles, 28 floors, day 44).
5. **The last line is the cliffhanger** ("Someone was already inside the fence."). No "tell me in the comments".
6. Characters speak short lines in their own voices (Part 2), between narration.
7. Beanie Survives adds real ingenuity: "Nobody can throw a rope 60 m. But this problem was solved in 1848: a boy
   with a kite carried the first line across Niagara." Clever, true, visual.
8. Titles: "<Disaster> at <very specific place>: Don't <the obvious thing>" and "30 Days Locked Inside a Supermarket".

## 4. What we were doing wrong
- Too long and too slow (2:00-2:30 at trailer pace). The story starts after a 3-second title card on Part 1.
- Hooks were a mystery question ("Trust him?") instead of a decision the viewer has a stake in.
- Scenes describe mood ("The temperature dropped. Ten degrees. Then zero.") instead of decisions and consequences.
- 5-8 seconds of each part is spent asking for comments out loud.
- One survival rule per story; the outliers pack 5-8 small smart ideas into a minute.

## 5. What changes now (applied from Saturday's "Last Wave")
- **Fast format**: 55-80 s per part, 150-190 words, narration ~15% faster with 60% shorter pauses
  (new `"pace"` setting in the story file; src/voice.py). A scene change every ~3 s.
- **Hook = a decision**: "If zombies trap you in a beach town, don't run for the roads. Run for the water."
- No title card on Part 1 (`"title_card": false`); the end card is one short line after the cliffhanger.
- Scripts are a chain of decision -> consequence -> new problem, with numbers; at least 4 "smart moves" per part.
- Titles keep the proven "<Threat>: ... Trapped In <place>" pattern, adding "Don't <obvious thing>" where it fits.
- Still 3 parts of one story a day, starring Kai, survival + horror (owner's fixed decisions).

## 6. Ideas not done yet (owner's call)
- Character dialogue in separate voices (the outliers use it). Needs a second and third voice set up.
- One continuing story world across days ("Day 1... Day 30") instead of a new story daily: the 7.2M Part 1 fed
  every later part (436K, 291K...). We could keep 3 parts a day but make each day the next chapter of Kai's outbreak.
- A second, cheap format for reach: 30-40 s absurd "Top 10 ___ to survive ___" rankings or "You're in a ___ movie".
